<p>
  Panama's Investment Outlook examines Panama as a connected hub economy and places the
  Qualified Investor Visa in that wider setting. The report separates historical outcomes,
  current rules, planned projects, and forecasts. It is written for readers who want market
  context before choosing a residency investment.
</p>

<p>
  The paper's research proposition treats Panama's investment case as a connected system:
  dollarization, the Canal, ports, aviation, free-zone and business services, financial
  intermediation, fiscal capacity, institutional execution, and legally distinct investor
  mobility pathways. The abstract states a central conclusion of conditional resilience.
  Recent fiscal and Canal recovery strengthen the near-term picture, while durability depends
  on credible fiscal execution, water security delivery, transparent logistics, and financial
  integrity.
</p>

<p>
  On the macro side, the report cites Panama Canal Authority FY2025 preliminary results,
  including B/.5.705 billion in revenue, B/.4.134 billion in net profit, 13,404 vessel
  transits, and 489.1 million CP/SUAB tons. It also cites MEF-reported figures for a
  non-financial public-sector deficit decline from 6.23% of GDP in 2024 to 3.68% in 2025,
  and 4.8% year-over-year GDP growth in the first quarter of 2026. Those figures are
  presented as historical or institutional results in the paper, not as guarantees of future
  performance.
</p>

<p>
  The residence section stresses a practical limit: obtaining residency in Panama does not
  automatically establish tax residency. It also does not guarantee bank-account access,
  school or university admission, citizenship, or a financial return on the investment.
  The Qualified Investor pathway is described as permanent residence through a qualifying
  investment, within a broader immigration system that also includes employment, family,
  demographic, and Friendly Nations routes.
</p>
